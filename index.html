About

Saul Anton is a critic and theorist whose work explores the intersection of aesthetics, political thought, and critical theory. He writes on the relationship between artistic form and political authority—how images, texts, and objects both stage and fracture the claims of power—from the French Enlightenment to contemporary art and media. His current book project, Sovereignty in Ruins, traces an alternative genealogy of political authority through aesthetic form, from Versailles and Diderot’s fragments to photography and contemporary continental thought.

He is Associate Professor at Pratt Institute and holds a Ph.D. in French Literature from Princeton University. His writing has appeared in Artforum, frieze, Afterall, Parallax, and Bookforum, among other publications. He is the translator of Jean-Luc Nancy’s Discourse of the Syncope (Stanford University Press) and the author of Lee Friedlander’s Little Screens (MIT Press) and Warhol’s Dream.

Recent grants and fellowships include a National Endowment for the Humanities Summer Research Stipend (2024), a J. Paul Getty Center Library Research Award (2024), and a Visiting Scholarship at the American Academy in Rome (2026).

Selected Writing
Book in Progress
Sovereignty in Ruins: Aesthetic Form and the Afterlives of Political Authority

Sovereignty in Ruins argues that political sovereignty has never existed apart from the aesthetic, medial, and linguistic forms through which it is staged, fractured, and sustained. Against genealogies that route modern political thought through German Idealism, it traces an alternative path through France as the first modern absolute state. From Machiavelli and Hobbes to Diderot and Rousseau, from ruins in the French Revolution to photography and contemporary theory, the book asks how authority persists not by securing stable foundations but by managing its own exposure—through literature, art, architecture, and modern media of witnessing.
