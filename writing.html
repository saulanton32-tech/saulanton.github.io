Book in Progress
Sovereignty in Ruins: Aesthetic Form and the Afterlives of Political Authority

Sovereignty in Ruins argues that political sovereignty has never existed apart from the aesthetic, media, and linguistic forms through which it is staged, fractured, and sustained. In contrast to traditional accounts of modern political theory, the book traces an alternative genealogy through France—the first modern absolute state—from Machiavelli and Hobbes to Diderot and Rousseau, to the ruin paintings of Hubert Robert, the architecture of Claude-Nicolas Ledoux, and to photography, contemporary theory, and the politics of witnessing. It asks how authority persists not by securing stable foundations but by managing its own exposure in works of art, architecture, and literature—from ruin paintings to photography to the modern politics of witnessing.
